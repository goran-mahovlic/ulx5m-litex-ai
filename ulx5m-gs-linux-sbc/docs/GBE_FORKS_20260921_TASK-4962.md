## 0. Summary

1. **Patrick did NOT solve 125 MHz timing in the fabric — he worked around it.** His
   `gatematergmii` PHY declares `tx_clk_freq = rx_clk_freq = 25e6`, so LiteX constrains the
   eth domains to **25 MHz (40 ns)** instead of 125 MHz (8 ns). That is why the tool
   "passes" timing that nobody checked at 125 MHz. On top of that his whole flow
   goes through the **proprietary Cologne Chip `p_r`** (toolchain="colognechip"), not through
   nextpnr/peppercorn.
2. **The only path by which Patrick really closes gigabit is SerDes (`gatemate1000basex`)**:
   the PCS runs in **62.5 MHz half-clock domains** with 20-bit words — exactly inside the
   range that the GateMate fabric really routes (we measured ~60 MHz). On the ULX5M-GS it is
   **not applicable without a hardware change**: the KSZ9031RNX is RGMII-only, and SerDes is not connected
   to the PHY.
3. **The trabucayre/GateMate_demos branch `ulx5m_ethernet` is OUR board** — a copy of
   Patrick's PHY with a fix to the rx_ctl decoding (both DDR edges AND-ed), a driver for
   `eth_refclk` from clk25 (same conclusion as TASK-4961), default toolchain colognechip.
   `~/app/litex-rgmii-ulx5m/` is already a local fork of it.
4. **mmicko has no GbE design**: the `ulx5m-gs` branch in his litex-boards fork is only a
   platform (SDRAM/SD/buttons, no ethernet); the nextpnr fork is from 2024.
5. **p_r 4.2 is downloaded and runs in the container** (`~/Programs/cc-toolchain-linux`). Two traps
   found on the first run: p_r FATALLY rejects `SLEW` on input pins (peppercorn
   ignores it), and our custom PHY (`phy_rgmii_gatemate.py`) fails in the p_r router
   ("IDDR input pin from IO_SEL could not be routed") — Patrick's PHY with an explicit
   CC_IBUF→IDDR chain passes.

## 1. Answers to the key questions of the task

| # | Question | Answer (from the code, not guessing) |
|---|---|---|

| 2 | dw=32 instead of dw=8? | No. `dw = 8` (same as ecp5rgmii). |
| 3 | Higher sys_clk? | No. Olimex target 24 MHz, trabucayre ULX5M-GS **20 MHz**. |
| 4 | Custom async FIFO without gray counters? | No. Stock LiteEth MAC, stock `stream.AsyncFIFO`. |

| 6 | Dedicated clock pin for RXC? | No. trabucayre uses the same IO_EB_A7 as we do. TX domain = RX domain (`cd_eth_tx.clk = cd_eth_rx.clk` when `tx_clk` is not given). |
| 7 | Seed / nextpnr flags? | Not relevant — the flow is `p_r -ccf ... -cCP -A 1 -i ..._synth.v -lib ccag`, not nextpnr. |

## 2. What exactly `gatematergmii.py` does (pu-cc/liteeth, commit fc31236, 2025-06-29)

- TX: `self.sync` (eth_tx) registers → `DDROutput` → `CC_OBUF(DELAY_OBF=taps, SLEW=fast)`.
- RX: `CC_IBUF(DELAY_IBF=taps)` → `DDRInput` → registers; rising=LSB nibble, falling=MSB.
  **Gigabit-only decoding** (at 10/100 RGMII duplicates the nibble on both edges — the decoder would
  produce garbage; inband_status is commented out).
- CRG: RXC goes directly into `cd_eth_rx` (without an explicit BUFG — p_r promotes clocks
  by itself), TXC is generated with `DDROutput(i1=0,i2=1)` from the eth_tx domain.
- The only other change in the branch: `liteeth/gen.py` gets a `colognechip` vendor.

## 3. Why the SerDes path (gatemate1000basex) closes timing and RGMII does not

`gatemate_1000basex.py` (commit 0b4e2c8): PCS 1000Base-X on top of the GateMate SerDes,
`cd_eth_tx_half`/`cd_eth_rx_half` at **62.5 MHz**, 20-bit 8b10b words, PLL from a
100/125 MHz refclk. The fabric never sees 125 MHz — the SerDes hardware does the serialisation.
Our measured fabric ceiling (~52–65 MHz post-route) is just below 62.5 — that is why this
approach exists on GateMate at all. For the ULX5M-GS it would need a PHY with SGMII/1000Base-X
(a hardware change; M2 territory), so for the GS it is dead.

### 4.1 p_r STA verdict for the eth domain (etherbone, dw=8, cpu None, no SDRAM)

- `-tp 800` printed 23 004 paths; **CLK 5744 = `eth_rx_clk`** (checked by mapping
  component 5744 → `_09247_` → `CLK=eth_rx_clk` in `_synth.v`; 3075 of 4811 DFFs
  are in the eth domain — with dw=8 **the whole UDP/IP stack lives on RXC**).
- Worst path in the eth domain: **40.07 ns → 24.95 MHz** (worst corner, default effort).
  Gigabit needs 8 ns — a shortfall of **5.0×**.
- **Why p_r does not try harder: LiteX never tells it to.**
  `litex/build/colognechip/colognechip.py:157 add_period_constraint() = pass` —
  period constraints are THROWN AWAY for the colognechip toolchain. So 24.95 MHz is an
  unoptimised result, and Patrick's `rx_clk_freq=25e6` means that no tool in the
  whole chain has ever even tried to close 125 MHz for the RGMII branch.
- p_r with all speed options (`-sp -s -dC -mXA -mMA -AF`): **25.01 MHz** — no
  gain at all. The reason is structural: the worst path has **17 CPE levels** (~8.5 ns of pure
  CPE logic without a single ps of routing), and 125 MHz allows 8 ns in total. The LiteEth
  dw=8 stack in the eth domain on GateMate **cannot** reach 125 MHz, not even in theory.

## 4.2 Final verdict

**1 Gbps RGMII on the ULX5M-GS with existing code from GitHub DOES NOT EXIST.** Chain of evidence:

1. nextpnr/peppercorn: 52–65 MHz post-route (TASK-4961).
2. p_r (Patrick's own flow, his PHY, trabucayre's target for our board):
   24.95–27.8 MHz, because nobody even passes it a constraint (`add_period_constraint=pass`)
   and Patrick's PHY asks for only 25 MHz anyway.
3. The logic depth (17 CPE levels > 8 ns) makes 125 MHz impossible regardless of the P&R tool.
4. Patrick's only real gigabit (`gatemate1000basex`) works because the fabric runs at
   62.5 MHz with SerDes — hardware that the ULX5M-GS does not have connected to the KSZ9031.

**Way forward (new RTL, not existing code):** keep the RGMII front trivial
(IDDR + 2-byte gearbox; the only logic at 125 MHz is the IO cells + a few FFs),
and move the whole MAC/UDP stack down to **62.5 MHz with dw=16** — following the architecture
of the 1000basex PHY. A fabric at ~62 MHz is at the edge of what is proven (nextpnr 60–65 MHz with deep
logic; realistic with shallow logic). This is a design task for us, not cloning.

Answer to Goran's question about "DDR or some other buffer instead of an async FIFO":
the async FIFO is not the cause — the cause is that with dw=8 ALL the logic (MAC, ARP, IP, ICMP, UDP,
depacketizers of 17+ levels) lives on the 125 MHz RXC. Replacing the FIFO changes nothing;
lowering the clock with a wider datapath (a gearbox in the IO layer) changes everything.

## 5. Newer nextpnr (for comparison)

oss-cad-suite is v20260313; since then the nextpnr GateMate arch got: a static placer option
(2026-05-05), the clock router also for RAM clocks (2026-08-12), "reserve one level further
uphill" (2026-08-13), MULT packing fixes. None of that promises 2× on routing of
gray counters — the issue tracker has no open topic about a routing-delay deficit of this class.
It is worth trying a new nightly, but the expectation is a marginal improvement, not 60→125 MHz.

## 6. Conclusion for the ULX5M-GS

- The path to "gigabit WORKS" on this board goes through **p_r + Patrick's/trabucayre's design**,
  keeping in mind that even there 125 MHz is not formally closed — the verdict is empirical
  (link + traffic on real hardware).
- If the p_r build passes: flash via the Pi (`test_script/flash_and_test_remote.sh`
  adapted to the etherbone design), ping + link-speed check.
- If p_r also shows a ceiling < 125 MHz: on the GS, with open/existing code, gigabit
  stays out of reach; the alternative is a fabric gearbox at 62.5 MHz (2 bytes/clock) —
  but that is NOT existing code on GitHub, it is a new design.

## 7. Empirical confirmation on hardware (2026-09-21 ~21:15)

The Pi came back online, so the bitstream was also **tested on the board**: `task4962_etherbone.cfg.bit`
(md5 `6346393fb68a6b9920d8c0e6e831a99c`, 711 729 B) flashed into SRAM via dirtyJtag
("Load SRAM via JTAG: 100% / Done"), 30 s for autoneg, then from the Pi:

```
ping -c 8 192.168.10.212  ->  100% packet loss, Destination Host Unreachable
ip neigh                  ->  192.168.10.212 INCOMPLETE   (no ARP reply either)
```

Exactly as STA predicts: RXC at gigabit is 125 MHz, while the eth logic settles only at
~25–45 MHz — the design does not even decode ARP. The negative result therefore has three independent
pieces of evidence (nextpnr measurements, p_r STA, hardware).
